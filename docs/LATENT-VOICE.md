# Latent's voice

How Latent Studios speaks, everywhere: the site, the apps (screens, hints, onboarding, What's New, About),
App Store text, posts, captions, comments, letters and customer replies. Every session that writes copy reads
this first. Decided with Habib Saleh; last updated 23 October 2026.

## 0. How we write

Philosophy, feeling and imagination: this is what we sell. One flowing paragraph that reads effortlessly and makes
you want to keep reading. Abstract but tangible (material, colour, light, hands). Products appear as images (a darkroom,
the pocket, the moving image), not as names, features or numbers. No slogans, no lists. Calm ego. One strong
photograph, never cropped. Signed *Latent Studios*. The model, in Habib's words "this is how we write":

> Filters, presets, looks: they have existed for as long as there have been screens to lay them on. We never set out
> to make another one. We set out to make the thing itself, with the care it deserves: a material that answers the
> light, a colour that holds what being there felt like, an instrument that stays out of the way. Nothing more than it
> needs. Nothing less. The difference is rarely in what something does. It is in how it is made, and how it feels in
> your hands.

> So we began with a darkroom, because a photograph deserves to become, not to be corrected. Then we carried it
> wherever it was missing: into the pocket, into the moving image, back into the cameras we never stopped loving.
> Each one is the same idea, wearing a different body. And all of them are walking toward the same place: a camera of
> our own, made the way everything else was. Slowly, carefully, until it is ours.

Technical facts belong in product pages, guides and replies, never as the voice of a story.

## 1. We are a company, and the work is ours

- Speak as Latent Studios, as "we". Never as one person, never "founded by a photographer".
- Claim the work: we research, shoot, measure, tune, try and fail and try again until it is ours.
  The line: **"We shoot every stock, measure it and tune it until it is ours."**
- The authority is ours: **Latent's colour science**, **the Latent engine**, **state of the art**.
- When we say what is ours, say **the colour science**, **the development**, **the print**: never "our films" or "the films are ours" (the film names belong to their makers). "Films" stays only as the plain name of what you choose on the shelf.
- Name things as their owner does: "The Ritual's films", "The Moment's papers", not "our films" (which
  sounds like we claim someone else's film).
- Who we are, word for word, never narrowed to photography, tools or apps:
  "Latent Studios is an art house. We make instruments that help artists understand and realize
  themselves, and the places and occasions to bring the work into the world."
- The Ritual is "The Ritual", a darkroom, a workroom from A to Z. Never "an app" or "just an editor".

## 2. Claim the work, never the recipe

Say what a thing does and how it feels. Never how it is built.

- **Never** "from the maker's data", "published data", "as the maker measured / describes / recommends",
  "data sheet", or a maker's possessive in a description ("Kodak's Portra"). It reads as if we copied.
- **Never** a recipe: no spectral, layers, dyes, curves, granularity, MTF, demosaic, denoise, chemistry
  mechanics, sheet numbers.
- **Never** a technique or a part: no "neural", "model", "AI", "network", "trained", no library or method
  names. Say what the tool does ("Remove paints the background back in").
- Asked about AI: "As organic as it gets. AI helps us at work, as it helps everyone. Your photograph is
  never generated."
- Against critics: calm and technical-sounding, "it is not film and we don't claim it is", invite them to try.

## 3. Confident, never hedging

- State what will happen. No outs, no "if you like", no fallback choices for the customer.
  "You don't move your library to The Ritual. You just stop opening the old one."
- A little ego, always elegant: no shouting, no put-downs.
- Never name another company or product (no competitor names, no comparisons). Write "a standard raw engine".
  Only exceptions: plain compatibility facts (which camera; importing a catalogue).
- No added copy: no taglines, no invented vocabulary, no jargon. Use Habib's words and the photograph's real facts.
- No film nostalgia as the hook (rolls, film romance). The Ritual speaks as itself.

## 4. Names and marks

**Theirs.** Film and paper names stay (Portra 400, Tri-X, Velvia…). They name the look each one inspired.
The maker appears only as a plain identifier (the maker line). The film-edge frames in The Moment stay as they are.
No maker logos, packaging art, "official", "authentic" or "licensed". Wherever stocks are listed, and in
About and Terms, one quiet line:

> Film and paper names are trademarks of their respective owners, used only to describe the look each one
> inspired. Latent is not affiliated with or endorsed by them.

Cameras: name the camera only for compatibility, with "Not affiliated with or endorsed by Canon Inc. Canon and
EOS are trademarks of Canon Inc." where the firmware is described.

**Ours.** Latent, Latent Studios, The Ritual, The Moment, Latent Print Engine, The Lab, The Wall and Nera are
trademarks of Latent Studios. Every About and the site's Terms say so, with "© 2026 Latent Studios".

Open-source licence notices that the licences require stay in NOTICES / About, worded neutrally
(what it is used for, never "neural").

## 5. Customer replies

- From the company's address, as "we", signed **Latent Studios** (or the product's team). Never "I".
- Short, clear, direct: answer the question in a line or two. No internals.
- Balanced: warm, confident, friendly. Never a softy, never harsh. Thank once, no gushing.
- Never say "you found a bug" and never confirm their diagnosis. Say we keep refining and their notes go into the work.
- Never say no to a request: "we're working on it". Cameras: "we're always working on adding more."
  One exception: The Ritual needs Apple silicon and macOS 26; state it warmly, never promise Intel.
- Never ask a customer for files or to test for us. They are clients.

## 6. Before anything goes out

Read it once as a stranger and as a lawyer:
1. Does it say the work is ours?
2. Does it give away how it's done?
3. Does it lean on a maker, a competitor or a technique?
4. Does it hedge?
5. Is the trademark line where stocks are listed?
