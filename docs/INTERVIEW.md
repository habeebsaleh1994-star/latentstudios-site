# Latent Studios: the founder interview

3 September 2026. Habib Saleh, interviewed by Claude. His words kept as
said, lightly punctuated. This is the source for the identity, the film,
and the site. Nothing here is marketing copy; it is what the studio is.

---

## 1. Why "Latent"?

> It explains itself, I think. The problem with everything modern, software
> and editing software, is that they treat an image as something not
> complete, something that needs to be corrected. Latent is the origin of
> the image, even almost before the image is printed. So it kind of implies
> that the image is born even before it is shot.

*Note:* the site says the photograph exists before development. Habib
says it exists before it is shot. Latency moves out of the darkroom and
into the world: the image is waiting in the street, in the light, in the
person. The camera does not make it and the software does not finish it.
Both are ways of arriving at something already whole.

## 2. Why a studio, and not just the products?

> The studio was made because I felt that the current configuration, for
> what I have in mind for Latent, is too restrained. Latent Studios can
> become a production house. It can give workshops. It can be an
> experimental lab for photography. It can create cameras. It can do
> anything art. It can do writing and books. It can do even designs. It can
> do art services.
>
> It started as me as a person, habibsaleh.com. Then it became products:
> Latent the Ritual, and the Moment, and the Lab. And then Versos, the
> prize. But it will keep growing. So there is a vision that cannot be
> contained, and Latent as a word works on all of these.
>
> Latent is like from scratch, and born before the act. It is born as a
> thought, as a feeling, as a spiritual experience.

*Note:* the order matters: a person, then instruments, then a prize, then
whatever is next. The studio is the container for growth that the
products could not hold. "Born before the act" is the whole identity in
four words: it applies to a photograph, a camera, a book, a workshop, a
design. The film should be about what exists before the act.

## 3. Which of those is closest, and which do you believe in most?

> I think I am the Dyson of this field.

*Note:* not a brand of looks; an engineering house. Dyson rebuilt an
ordinary object from physics, made thousands of prototypes, and owns the
instrument as well as the design. Read against the products: film as
behaviour rather than a look, a spectral engine, an emulsion modelled from
its dye response and its failures. Latent Studios is the studio that
builds the instruments of photography from first principles, and keeps
experimenting. "The experiments continue" is not a tagline; it is the
method.

## 4. The vacuum cleaner: the ordinary thing you refuse

> The same as how Latent works. The cameras should work like that too:
> invert the raw to negative, shoot negative, and print. Like a negative
> film, digitally.

*Note:* the refusal is the digital camera's idea of a finished picture. A
sensor gives you a positive, "correct" image and everything after is
correction. Habib's camera would give you a negative, the way film did:
something that has to be developed and printed, that carries the process
in it. This is the Dyson object: not a better filter, a different camera.
The Moment on iPhone is the first version of it (First Light is the
negative you interpret, never a raw to be fixed). A physical camera is
the destination.

## 5. What should they feel?

> Calm, nostalgic, curious, excited, warm, a bit scared maybe. Scared that
> this will take over the camera world and the whole art world, maybe?
> Power too. Generosity. Proud. What else?

*Note:* two families of feeling that usually don't sit together. Calm,
warm, nostalgic, generous on one side. Curious, excited, powerful, a
little frightening on the other. The first is the paper and the serif we
already have. The second is what the current site lacks entirely, and
what the film must carry: the sense that something large is coming into
view. Not menace. Scale. A tide, not a monster. Dyson, again: quiet
objects that changed an industry.

## 6. References outside photography

> unsplash.com/@europeana and unsplash.com/@clevelandart, I love these for
> example. I also love Barry Lyndon. As a book I like mine, habibsaleh.com.
> I like texture and imperfections, but in an elegant and harmonious and
> effortless way.

*Note:* Europeana is Europe's cultural-heritage archive: batik and
textiles, engravings, plates, the surfaces of old things photographed
close. The Cleveland Museum of Art's account is centuries of paintings,
prints and sketches, figurative and classical. Barry Lyndon is candlelight
and daylight only, compositions built like paintings, slow zooms out,
patience. His own book is paper, hairlines, a living manuscript.

The common temperature: the archive, not the feed. Surfaces with history.
Natural light. Painterly stillness. Texture and imperfection carried
with elegance, never as grunge. This is the visual world of Latent
Studios: it looks like a museum's open collection and a candlelit
interior, not like a software company. The film's material should be
drawn from this: plates, fabrics, pigments, paper fibre, candle-warm
light, and the frame among them.

## 7. The founder in the frame

> It can outlive me, but it is also me. It is my legacy, I think. I keep
> sending you my website so you can see that it is too harmonious with
> what Latent Studios stand for. So me and Latent are one.

*Note:* both at once, and he means it. The studio should be built to
outlive him, and it should be unmistakably his while he is here. The
resolution is the one publishers and ateliers found: the house carries
the founder's sensibility as its constitution, not his portrait as its
logo. habibsaleh.com is not a reference for the studio; it is the same
world at a different scale. The book and the studio share one
temperature, and the studio site should feel like the book grew a
workshop, not like a company acquired an artist.

## 8. Beirut

> No, I don't care about it. I am a child of the world.

*Note:* the city stays off the identity. No "Beirut" in the footer, no
regional framing, no origin story built on place. The studio belongs to
whoever looks, anywhere. The homepage eyebrow that says "Beirut" should
go; the archive-of-the-world references in question 6 are the true
geography.

---

## What the interview says, in one page

- **The belief:** the image is born before the act. Not before
  development; before the shutter. It waits in the world.
- **The studio:** a container for a vision that cannot be held by
  products. A person, then instruments, then a prize, then whatever comes:
  production, workshops, an experimental lab, cameras, books, design.
- **The method:** Dyson. Rebuild the ordinary object from first
  principles, prototype without end, own the instrument and the design.
  The experiments continue.
- **The refusal:** the digital camera's finished positive. The object to
  build: a camera that gives a negative, to be developed and printed. The
  Moment is its first form.
- **The feeling:** calm, warm, nostalgic, generous, and at the same time
  curious, excited, powerful, a little frightening. Scale without menace.
- **The visual world:** the archive, not the feed. Museum collections,
  engravings and textiles, candlelight and daylight, paper and hairlines.
  Texture and imperfection carried with elegance.
- **The founder:** one with the studio while here; the studio built to
  outlive him. Sensibility as constitution, not portrait as logo.
- **Place:** none. A child of the world.

## Postscript, the brief in one line

> Maybe Latent Studios should be like if Barry Lyndon had a website, and
> blend with these: clevelandart and europeana.

*Note:* candlelight and daylight, compositions built like paintings,
patience; blended with the open archives of a museum and of Europe's
heritage: engravings, plates, textiles, sketches on paper. Both archives
are free to use and alter (Cleveland's open access is public domain;
Europeana's Unsplash images carry the Unsplash licence). Not a gimmick,
not a slope: the material of the identity is the archive coming alive.
