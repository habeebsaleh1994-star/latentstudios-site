What you need

Made for how artists actually work.

Not features. The things you have had to work around everywhere else, each already built and waiting in the sites above, and none of them asking anything of you until you reach for it.

One

You already sequenced it. In Lightroom, last night.

Drop the folder. The story arrives in the order you took the pictures, with the titles and captions you already wrote in the files. Nothing is retyped. Your first site can begin this way, before you have chosen anything.

Two

A collector asks what paper it is on.

Every print has a back: where it was made, a line in your hand, the edition, the paper. A visitor turns it over. You write on it in place, once.

Three

The series is not ready. The gallery is.

A page, or the whole site, behind a word you give to whom you choose; or a holding page before you open. A page behind a word is sealed in the files, not hidden: nothing of it can be read without the word.

Four

A sixty-centimetre print is sixty centimetres.

Give a work its real size and it can be seen at its centimetres on the screen, matched once to a bank card held against it. A painter's wall hangs at true relative scale.

Five

Change your mind without losing a thing.

Editing is always a draft; publishing is a decision. Every published version is kept and any can be put back. Change the template and every photograph and word stays where it was.

Each of the nineteen makes the big decisions for a kind of artist, so you are not flooded with them. Point at a name to see it running; open one to try it with your own work.
