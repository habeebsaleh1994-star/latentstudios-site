Latent Wall. For the first artists

Thank you for looking first.

Latent Wall makes a website for a body of work: photographs, paintings, prints, films, writing, projects. You bring a folder; it becomes a site in one of eighteen templates, each with its own looks; you change what you like and publish when it is a decision, not a draft.

You are seeing it before anyone else. It is finished enough to live with, and not yet finished. What we want from you is simple: make your site as if it were real, and tell us everything that got in the way.

Begin at the arrival page

How it works, in brief

  1. Choose a template on the start page. Each is for a kind of artist and makes the big decisions; you can change it later without losing anything.
  2. Bring a folder. Photographs, drawings, scans. JPEG, PNG or HEIC, up to 15 MB each; a film up to 400 MB. Titles and dates come from the files when they have them.
  3. Edit on the page. Titles, captions, the words of the front page, are typed where they are. The panel on the right holds the rest: pages, order, the back of each print, who can see what.
  4. Customise the look, the faces, day and night, and the details. Every choice there changes something you can see; if one seems not to, tell us.
  5. Publish when it is ready. It makes the whole site as files.

Where your site lives, for now

During this test your site lives in the browser you make it in, on your own device. Nothing is sent anywhere until you publish, and publishing makes files, not a live address. Two things follow from that:

  • Use one browser on one device for your site. Clearing that browser's data would lose the draft.
  • When you publish, send us the files. We put them at the address we have agreed with you, the same day, and tell you when they are up. Your own domain comes with the release.

What to tell us

Anything, but these most of all:

  • A moment you did not know what to do next.
  • A choice that did nothing, or did something you did not expect.
  • A word that was wrong for your work: a label, a hint, a button.
  • A page that looked less than your work deserves, on your laptop or on your phone.
  • Something you needed and could not find.

A screenshot with a sentence is perfect. Write to hello@latentstudios.art.

Known, for now
